When people talk about infrastructure, they usually mean roads, ports and power. But some of Africa's most powerful infrastructure is invisible: **the stories, sounds and styles** that shape how the continent is seen, and how it sees itself.

## A sector hiding in plain sight

Africa's creative industries already reach global audiences. Yet they are often treated as entertainment rather than enterprise, and left out of investment strategies, policy frameworks and development programmes.

## What changes when culture is strategic

- **Identity becomes an asset.** Places and brands with a strong cultural story attract talent, tourism and investment.
- **Creators become employers.** Supported properly, creative ventures scale into businesses with real supply chains.
- **Communities gain a voice.** Arts and public engagement build the trust that every other programme depends on.

## How to act on it

Institutions can embed culture into strategy by funding creative projects with clear business models, building partnerships between creators and corporates, and designing public engagements that are owned by the communities they serve.

Strategy speaks to the head. Culture speaks to the heart. The most durable change needs both.

## The economics of creativity

Creative work is often described in terms of passion. It should also be described in terms of value chains. A single music release or film involves writers, producers, technicians, marketers, distributors, venues and merchandisers. A design or fashion label touches manufacturing, retail and export. When these chains are strengthened, employment and income follow.

## Three barriers worth removing

### Access to appropriate finance

Creative ventures are often too small or too unconventional for traditional lenders. Blended finance, revenue-based models and grant-to-investment pathways can bridge the gap.

### Intellectual property and fair returns

Without clear rights management, creators struggle to capture the value they generate. Education, legal support and collective management structures make a measurable difference.

### Market and audience connection

Talent is abundant; routes to audiences are not. Partnerships with platforms, brands, festivals and diaspora networks can open markets at home and abroad.

## Culture in every strategy

You do not need to be a culture ministry to act on this. Banks can design creative-sector products. Corporates can commission local artists and storytellers. Development programmes can use arts and public engagement to build trust and participation. Cities can invest in cultural districts as engines of regeneration.

When culture is treated as infrastructure, it stops being a line item and becomes a platform for identity, enterprise and belonging.
