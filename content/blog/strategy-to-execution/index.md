There is a familiar moment in many organisations: the strategy is approved, the slides are beautiful, and six months later very little has changed. The plan was not wrong. It was simply **never designed to be executed**.

## Where the gap opens

The distance between strategy and execution usually comes down to a few predictable failures:

- Priorities that are too many to be priorities at all.
- Owners who are named but not resourced.
- Metrics that measure activity instead of outcomes.
- No rhythm for reviewing progress and adjusting course.

## Designing for delivery

At The Plug Afrique, we treat execution as part of strategy, not a phase that follows it.

### Start with fewer, sharper choices

A strategy with three clear choices beats one with twelve aspirations. Clarity is a gift to the people who have to deliver.

### Translate choices into operating rhythms

Every priority needs an owner, a budget, a forum where it is reviewed, and a definition of "done".

### Make progress visible

Simple, shared dashboards build momentum and surface problems while they are still cheap to fix.

## The human factor

Plans are executed by people. Engagement, capability and communication are not soft add-ons. They are the delivery mechanism. **Insight must meet execution**, and that meeting happens in conversations, not documents.

## A simple test for any strategy

Before you approve your next strategy, ask five questions:

1. **Can every leader state the three priorities from memory?** If not, there are too many, or they are too vague.
2. **Does each priority have a named owner with a real budget?** Ownership without resources is a wish.
3. **What will we stop doing?** Strategy is as much about subtraction as addition.
4. **How will we know we are on track in 90 days?** Leading indicators beat annual reviews.
5. **Who on the frontline has been consulted?** The people closest to customers often see the obstacles first.

## From plan to rhythm

Execution lives in calendars, not documents. The most effective organisations we work with build a simple operating rhythm:

- **Weekly:** team-level check-ins on actions and blockers.
- **Monthly:** priority reviews with owners, looking at outcome metrics and resourcing.
- **Quarterly:** a strategic review that is willing to change course when the evidence says so.

This rhythm turns strategy from an event into a habit.

## When to bring in support

External partners add the most value at three moments: when priorities need to be sharpened, when cross-functional or multi-stakeholder alignment is stuck, and when delivery capability needs to be built quickly. In each case the goal is the same: to leave the organisation more capable of executing on its own.

Good strategy is not the plan that looks best on paper. It is the plan that gets done.
