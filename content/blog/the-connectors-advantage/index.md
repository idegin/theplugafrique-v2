Africa does not have a shortage of ambition. Founders are building, governments are reforming and investors are looking. Yet many promising initiatives stall, not for lack of money or talent, but because **the right people never meet at the right moment**.

## The missing middle is connection

Across sectors, we see the same pattern. A ministry designs a programme that a private operator could deliver better. A venture needs a distribution partner who is already serving its customers. A foundation funds a pilot that nobody is positioned to scale.

Each of these is a connection problem. Solving it requires three things:

1. **A map:** knowing who holds which capability, mandate or asset.
2. **Trust:** a neutral broker who can align interests without competing for them.
3. **Structure:** agreements, governance and incentives that keep partners together after the launch photo.

## Why connectors create outsized value

When a connection works, value compounds. A single well-structured partnership can unlock financing, market access and policy support at the same time. That is why ecosystem builders, the people and organisations who make introductions *and* make them stick, have become some of the most valuable actors in African markets.

> Progress happens when the right people and ideas meet.

## What this means for leaders

- **Audit your network as a strategic asset.** Who could unlock your next stage, and how far are you from them?
- **Design partnerships for year three, not week one.** Governance and shared metrics matter more than the MoU.
- **Invest in brokers.** Neutral convenors reduce friction for everyone at the table.

The organisations that win the coming decade will not only be the best capitalised. They will be the best *connected*.

## Three connections that change trajectories

In our work across sectors, three kinds of connection consistently unlock the most value.

### 1. Public meets private

Governments hold mandates, data and convening power. Private operators hold delivery capacity and capital. When the two are structured into the same programme, with clear roles, shared metrics and a fair allocation of risk, outcomes improve for citizens and investors alike. The hard part is rarely the idea. It is the translation between two very different institutional languages.

### 2. Venture meets ecosystem

Early-stage ventures often have strong products and weak distribution. Corporates and established institutions often have distribution and a need for innovation. A well-brokered introduction, followed by a pilot with real success criteria, can compress years of growth into months.

### 3. Local meets global

International partners entering African markets need local depth: relationships, context and credibility. African organisations scaling outward need global standards and networks. The connector sits in the middle, translating expectations in both directions.

## The discipline behind a good partnership

Connection is not just networking. Partnerships that last share a few disciplines:

| Discipline | What it looks like in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Shared outcomes | One page of agreed results, owned by every partner |
| Clear governance | Named decision-makers, a regular review rhythm, an escalation path |
| Fair value exchange | Each partner can say, in one sentence, what they gain |
| Exit and renewal | Agreed points to review, renew or responsibly unwind |

When these are designed in from the start, partnerships stop being fragile alliances and become durable infrastructure for growth.

## Where to start

Begin with the outcome you cannot achieve alone. Map who holds the missing pieces. Then invest in the conversation that brings them together, and in the structure that keeps them together.

That is the connector's advantage, and it is available to any leader willing to treat relationships as strategy.
